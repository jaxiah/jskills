# Michael Nielsen: Augmenting Long-term Memory

Source: Michael Nielsen, [Augmenting Long-term Memory](https://augmentingcognition.com/ltm.html), July 2018.

This is a selective reading guide, not a copy of the article. The original is the source for its examples and full argument. Nielsen presents much of his Anki practice as personal observation rather than controlled evidence.

## Practical Heuristics for Card Authors

These are defaults to adapt to the learner, not validity rules.

- **Build understanding in passes.** Learn accessible names and notation first, then ask deeper questions as the subject becomes familiar.
- **Select deliberately.** Prefer knowledge tied to current projects or durable interests; avoid accumulating disconnected questions for hypothetical future use.
- **Diagnose recall failures.** Split a repeatedly missed composite question into focused parts, but keep an integrative question when recalling the whole relationship is useful.
- **Build associations.** Relate new material to existing knowledge and let the learner inspect and personalize drafted questions.
- **Preserve epistemic status.** When evidence is uncertain, ask what a named source claims rather than presenting its claim as settled fact.
- **Improve weak prompts.** Treat bare yes/no questions as candidates for more informative distinctions; use figures when visual recall matters.
- **Prefer formulation over features.** Basic Q&A and cloze often suffice; better questions matter more than extra Anki features.
- **Respect the limits of cards.** Practice procedures in context, keep review volume sustainable, and do not turn Nielsen's personal numbers or deck choices into requirements.

## Why Memory Matters

- Spaced repetition makes long-term retention a deliberate choice. Its value is not limited to memorizing trivia: readily available knowledge supports reading, problem solving, and creative work.
- Basic names, notation, and concepts are often the bottleneck in a new field. Once they are familiar, higher-level material takes less effort to interpret.
- An internal network of knowledge allows faster association than repeatedly looking things up. External notes remain useful, but they serve a different purpose.

## Learning Through Repeated Passes

- In his AlphaGo reading example, Nielsen first collected easy background questions, then revisited the paper several times as more of it became understandable. Later questions addressed architecture, limitations, and deeper ideas rather than just terminology.
- A shallow read can capture a paper's central claims and useful figures. A deep read of a key paper builds a framework for judging related work; reading many papers at different depths gradually builds a picture of a field.
- Questions can change as understanding develops. A card that is useful at the beginning of a subject need not be the final form of that knowledge.

## Choosing What to Remember

- Prefer material connected to an active project or a durable interest. Striking facts may be worth keeping even when their future use is unclear, but collecting everything creates review cost without equivalent value.
- Isolated questions often lose their meaning when the original reading context fades. Link new material to existing knowledge and consider related prompts that establish its place in a larger idea.
- Separate a source's claim from an established fact. When the evidence is uncertain, frame a question around who made the claim and under what conditions instead of memorizing an unqualified assertion.

## Formulating Questions

- Focused questions make errors easier to diagnose. If a composite card is repeatedly missed, split it to expose the particular weak link.
- Splitting does not require discarding every integrative question. Smaller components can support a broader question that tests how they fit together; a high-level question can itself be focused when its prerequisites are familiar.
- Constructing and revising one's own prompts helps form associations. An agent's draft should therefore be treated as material for the learner to inspect and personalize, not a substitute for understanding.
- A bare yes/no prompt may deserve a question about the distinction, condition, or example behind the answer. Images and figures can also be worthwhile recall targets when their shape or meaning matters.
- Simple Q&A and cloze cover much of Nielsen's practice. Better question formulation matters more than exploring every available Anki feature.

## Boundaries of the Method

- Remembering a procedure does not by itself create procedural skill. Apply it in real tasks and solve problems with it.
- Card creation must remain compatible with a sustainable review habit; adding too quickly can create a discouraging backlog.
- Nielsen's timing estimates, card counts, deck organization, and personal quotas illustrate his practice; they are not universal requirements for ankify.
- The essay also distinguishes robust benefits of spaced practice from uncertain details of memory theory. Treat its practical patterns as useful hypotheses to test against the learner's results.
